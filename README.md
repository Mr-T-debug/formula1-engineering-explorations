# Formula 1 Engineering Explorations

Independent engineering investigations exploring the technical challenges of Formula 1 through mathematical modelling, simulation and computational analysis.

## Overview

Formula 1 represents some of the most challenging applications of engineering, where teams chase minute improvements in efficiency, poerformance, and reliability to stay ahead of the competition. This has become even more important as of recent, since regulation changes give rise to upgrades that could bring significant performance enhancements that can make a team with the fourth fastest car become the quickest in the space of just one weekend. 

This repository is a collection of independent engineering investigations exploring the technical challenges faced in Formula 1. Through mathematical modelling, numerical simulations and computational analysis, I aim to better understand the engineering principles behind the sport and how they influence vehicle performance. 

The projects are driven by curiosity and a desire to explore interesting engineering questions, ranging from power-unit performance and energy management to aerodynamics, vehicle dynamics, transmissions and control systems. I have been a fan of Formula 1 ever since I was 5, and aim to one day fulfil my goal of reaching the pinnacle of motorsport. 

Rather than simply explaining engineering concepts, the aim is to investigate them quantitatively, understand the underlying physics and explore the trade-offs involved in engineering design.

## Objectives

The primary objectives of these investigations are to:

* Apply fundamental engineering principles to relevant Formula 1 challenges.
* Develop computational models to investigate performance and design trade-offs.
* Explore how technical regulations influence engineering decisions.
* Improve my understanding of different areas of motorsport engineering.
* Share interesting findings in an accessible and reproducible format.

## Tools

Depending on the investigation, projects may involve:

* MATLAB: Mathematical modelling, numerical simulations and data analysis.
* Python: Computational analysis, optimisation and visualisation.
* Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD): Investigating aerodynamic and fluid-flow phenomena.
* Control Systems: Modelling and investigating dynamic systems and control strategies.

The tools and methods evolve as new projects are developed.

## About Me

I am a Mechanical Engineering Masters' Student at the King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) in Saudi Arabia. I previously completed a Mechatronics Engineering degree from the University of Cape Town. 

My engineering interests include powertrain systems, transmissions, vehicle dynamics, control systems and computational engineering.

This repository is an opportunity to continue exploring those interests through independent technical projects.

## Disclaimer

These projects are independent educational and engineering investigations.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the models use publicly available information, theoretical relationships and clearly identified assumptions. They do not represent official Formula 1 team simulations, proprietary engineering data or validated predictions of actual vehicle performance.

Formula 1 technical regulations may change, and regulatory assumptions should be interpreted with reference to the relevant published regulations.
